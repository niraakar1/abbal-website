About

An educator first — the counseling follows from that.

I work with a small number of students at a time, because the good stuff happens in the details: one essay, one score, one honest conversation.

My philosophy

Most students don't need to be pushed harder — they need someone to help them see the shape of what they're already capable of. My job is to make the path legible: to turn a vague, overwhelming process into a set of moves a sixteen-year-old can actually make.

“The goal isn't admission anywhere. It's a fit — a place where a student keeps becoming themselves.”

That's especially true for first-generation and South Asian students, who are often decoding American admissions and their own ambitions at the same time. I've lived close to that work — through the U.S. Embassy's guidance-counselor training, as an IC3 scholar, and as country director of Wonder Future Nepal — and I bring that whole context into the room.
